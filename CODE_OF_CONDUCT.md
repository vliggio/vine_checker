# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. This is a small hobby project; nobody here is getting paid, and nobody owes
you a fix on your schedule.

## What is expected

- Assume good faith. Most disagreements are a missing piece of context, not malice.
- Keep criticism on the code. "This breaks when a search returns zero items" is useful;
  remarks about the person who wrote it are not.
- Accept that maintenance is voluntary. Issues may sit. A feature may be declined
  because it is not worth the upkeep, which is a legitimate reason.
- Respect a decision once it has been made and explained, even if you disagree.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments — including about someone's identity,
  background, or experience level
- Unwelcome personal attention, or publishing anyone's private information
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or persistently reopening settled decisions
- Encouraging any of the above

## Scope

This applies to issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any other
project space, plus situations where someone is representing the project publicly.

## Enforcement

Report problems privately to the maintainer via
[GitHub](https://github.com/vliggio) rather than in the thread itself.

Reports are handled privately, and the reporter's identity is not shared. The maintainer
may edit or remove comments, close threads, or block accounts, in proportion to what
happened. Nobody is owed an appeal, but a good-faith explanation of a misunderstanding
will be listened to.

## Credit

This is a plain-language policy in the spirit of the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/), scaled to a one-person
project. If you need the formal text for policy reasons, treat the Contributor Covenant
v2.1 as the reference.
